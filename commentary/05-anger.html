Peterson's reading in plain words

"Thou shalt not kill" becomes "do not even be angry with your brother without cause." Peterson calls this one of the great turns in Western law: judgment moves from outcome to intent. Intent-based justice is harsher in one way, but it also makes mercy possible, because an accident is not a crime. He warns that accusations come back on the accuser, and that judging by impact alone wipes out the very idea of a defence.

Leaving your gift at the altar to reconcile with your brother is, he says, the root of his "clean your room" advice: fix what is close to home before you try to save the world. Agreeing with your adversary quickly means taking correction while you are still on the road together, because conflict put off is conflict multiplied. "Till thou hast paid the uttermost farthing" means you get away with nothing; reality is what you cannot distort.

Key ideas

From lecture section 17: Thou Shalt Not Kill, and Anger Without a Cause (Matthew 5:21-22)

  • Anger at your brother is judged like killing: justice moves from outcome to intent.
  • Accusers get accused; Peterson tweeted warnings that came true the same day.
  • Be judgmental in the discriminating sense; throwing out Nietzsche or Freud for one flaw justifies ignorance.
  • Intent-based justice allows mercy: an accident is not a crime.

From lecture section 18: Leave Thy Gift at the Altar: Reconcile With Thy Brother (Matthew 5:23-24)

  • Prepare yourself for proper sacrifice by doing small things close to home first.
  • Going to church can be a way to avoid reconciling with your brother.
  • Activism often means 'look how good I am'; it is convenient that Satan is somewhere that isn't you.
  • Solve your own moral problems first and you become able to solve family and social ones.

From lecture section 19: Agree With Thine Adversary Quickly and the Uttermost Farthing (Matthew 5:25-28)

  • Peterson admits he does not fully understand the verse and reasons it out: the adversary is testing you.
  • He would rather be corrected than right.
  • A messy house is littered with arguments that never happened; divorce drags 30,000 unsolved problems to the next partner.
  • There is a small proportion of truly exploitative people; most partners are no worse than you.
  • Marriage is Jung's alchemical container: because no one can run away, you can reveal yourself and not be alone in hell.
  • Reality is what you cannot distort; payment deferred is payment multiplied.
  • God tells Cain he made second-rate sacrifices and invited in sin crouching at the door.

Questions to explore

  • Whom have you accused that you could be accused of the same?
  • Where do you lean too far toward justice or mercy?
  • Which relationship should you fix before you claim to be good?
  • Whose approval are you still under, and why?
  • Which conflict are you deferring that is multiplying?
  • Where did you turn left when you knew you should turn right?

Peterson's reading in plain words

Peterson takes the blessings one at a time and reads each as a description of a person who is set up to grow.

Poor in spirit and meek both mean the opposite of narcissistic: humble enough to admit what you do not know. Security built on walls fails, he says, because the serpent always gets into the garden, so your stability has to rest on competence, and competence starts with making friends with the unknown. Pride of intellect, Milton's Lucifer, is the warning case. Those that mourn are people who let grief in fully; he tells how his father-in-law was transformed by years of caring for his dying wife, and says mourning is proportionate to love. Hunger and thirst after righteousness means wanting the right path enough to pay for it with work and truthful speech; what redeems a life is not happiness but its adventure. The merciful is a reciprocity idea: give a little more than is expected, be the 55-45 player, and doors open that you did not know existed. The pure in heart are free of contempt, including contempt for themselves, and admitting your faults is what brings relief. The persecuted are sharpened by opposition; ideas worth living by should be tested in battle.

Verse 9, "Blessed are the peacemakers", is not read aloud in the lecture; Peterson touches on peacemaking later, under loving your enemies.

Key ideas

From lecture section 2: Blessed Are the Poor in Spirit (Matthew 5:1-3)

  • Poor in spirit does not mean lacking energy; it means blessed are those who are the antithesis of narcissistic.
  • You know a place when what you do there works; the unknown is where your actions no longer match the situation.
  • The serpent always gets into the walled garden, so stability has to depend on competence, not security.

From lecture section 3: Blessed Are They That Mourn (Matthew 5:4)

  • Dell Roberts, an extroverted 'Dean Martin' type, unexpectedly became a devoted caregiver over 15 years of his wife's Alzheimer's-type illness.
  • Because he did not shield himself from grief, it transformed him and widened his character.
  • His wife suffered less; it was still hell, but not the lowest depths.

From lecture section 4: Blessed Are the Meek (Matthew 5:5)

  • Meek is a restatement of poor in spirit: not arrogant and narcissistic.
  • Milton portrays Lucifer, the light-bringer, as the highest angel gone wrong: rationality that elevates its own pretensions to the status of God.
  • The Tower of Babel is a structure built to replace God, and the result is that no one can communicate.

From lecture section 5: Hunger and Thirst After Righteousness (Matthew 5:6)

  • The impediments: you are bitter and resentful, you would rather aim down, and you want the benefits of being good without the work.
  • Pay attention to what you say and you can feel whether each word makes you weaker or stronger.
  • Work is sacrifice: it gives up the present for the future; not sacrificing still means sacrificing the future to the present.

From lecture section 6: Blessed Are the Merciful: Reciprocity (Matthew 5:7)

  • Dell's care for his wife led him to a new partner and to children who came to admire him.
  • Caring for the old is reciprocity with your future self: that's you, man.
  • If you deliver more than people expect, you get back more than you expect; work 10 percent longer and earn 40 percent more.

From lecture section 7: Blessed Are the Pure in Heart (Matthew 5:8)

  • Pure means not contaminated, not disgusting; it does not mean safe.
  • Eye-rolling contempt is the best predictor of divorce within a year.
  • Self-disgust tells you that you are doing something shameful; Peterson stopped drinking because he drank to misbehave with a good conscience.

From lecture section 8: Blessed Are the Persecuted (Matthew 5:10-12)

  • When you improve, people around you (a pathological family, drinking friends) push back.
  • A doubt only stops you if it echoes a doubt you already have; overcoming it strengthens commitment.
  • You do not want your six-year-old playing three-year-olds; opposition sharpens.

Questions to explore

  • What do you not know that might be the reason your life is not better?
  • Who in your life would you shield yourself from grieving, and what would it cost?
  • Which of your beliefs do you hold so tightly that you treat them as beyond correction?
  • What are you sacrificing right now, and to what?
  • Where could you be a 55-45 player this week?
  • What do you do that makes you disgusted with yourself?
  • Which criticism of you has a 5 percent you could use?

Peterson's reading in plain words

Wolves in sheep's clothing: Peterson says the most effective disguise of a predator is compassion. Narcissists and psychopaths use your compassion against you by claiming a moral virtue they have not earned.

"Ye shall know them by their fruits" he links to a rule from psychoanalysis: if you cannot understand someone's motive, look at the result and infer the aim. Once is an accident, twice a bad day, three times a pattern. Every good tree brings forth good fruit, so the whole tree has to be healthy. He describes a medieval painting in which Eve hands out apples of death beside a skull while Mary hands out the bread of life beside the cross, and draws the lesson that accepting death is the cure for death.

Verse 18, "A good tree cannot bring forth evil fruit", is not read aloud; its point is folded into his reading of verses 17 and 19-20.

Key ideas

From lecture section 45: Beware of False Prophets: Wolves in Sheep's Clothing (Matthew 7:15)

  • 'That's a good line for today.'
  • Sheep's clothing is harmlessness: 'I wouldn't hurt a fly.'
  • The most effective guise of a predator is compassion.
  • Narcissists and psychopaths use your compassion against you.

From lecture section 46: Ye Shall Know Them by Their Fruits: Aim From Consequence (Matthew 7:16)

  • The dictum is not 100 percent true but is a useful tool.
  • Once, accident. Twice, bad day. Three times, pattern. Four times, something in you aims for it.
  • Discipline rule of thumb: let it go once, give a break twice, speak up at three with three pieces of evidence.
  • If you cannot understand the motivation, look at the consequence and infer the cause.

From lecture section 47: Every Good Tree Bringeth Forth Good Fruit: The Tree Painting and Death (Matthew 7:17-20)

  • Getting the whole structure right: the whole tree healthy, the tree of life.
  • In the painting Eve hands out apples of death; Mary hands out the host; the crucifix is juxtaposed with the skull.
  • The acceptance of death is the cure for death; the antidote is incorporating willingness to accept the catastrophe of existence.
  • The painting sums up the entire biblical narrative in one image.

Questions to explore

  • Who uses compassion to gain moral status over you?
  • Do you ever wear sheep's clothing yourself?
  • What keeps happening to you, and what might it reveal about your aim?
  • What myth are you living out?
  • What fruit is your life bringing forth, honestly?
  • What part of the catastrophe of existence have you not yet accepted?

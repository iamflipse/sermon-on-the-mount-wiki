Fulfilling the Law

Matthew 5:17–20

Peterson's reading in plain words

Christ says he has come to fulfil the law, not destroy it. Peterson reads this as the partnership between two forces every person and society needs: the law (habit, tradition, what already works) and the prophet (the voice that updates it when the world changes). Neither is enough alone. Discipline is not the enemy of creativity; like a concert pianist, you go through discipline to reach freedom.

"One jot or one tittle" leads him to unfulfilled potential. What you fail to bring out of yourself becomes a source of guilt, and what bothers you most may be your calling. "Whosoever shall do and teach" puts doing first: belief shows in action, not in statements. Exceeding the scribes and Pharisees means being better than a clever lawyer or a showy priest, keeping the spirit of the law and not only its letter.

Key ideas

From lecture section 13: Think Not That I Am Come to Destroy the Law (Matthew 5:17)

  • Pageau: Barabbas was also called Jesus in one tradition, so the crowd chooses Jesus the revolutionary over Jesus the prophet.
  • The kingdom is universal, not political.
  • Your law is your habits; you also need the prophetic voice to update them as the world changes.
  • Conservatives and liberals play out the same law versus prophet tension.

From lecture section 14: One Jot or One Tittle: Potential and Responsibility (Matthew 5:18)

  • Gospel of Thomas: you will be destroyed by what you do not reveal within yourself.
  • Parents hate to see children betray who they could be, and discipline should stop it.
  • Potential is not actual, yet failing it produces real guilt and shame.
  • Alternative to Cain's complaint: the cosmos is flawed in proportion to your unwillingness to bring forth what is within you.

From lecture section 15: Whosoever Shall Do and Teach Them (Matthew 5:19)

  • 'Do and then teach': faith is not propositional assent; deciding to walk up the hill despite the evidence is the statement of faith.
  • Rogers (a missionary who lost his faith) taught congruence: what you do, feel, aim at and say all line up.
  • People worth listening to are the same in private and in public; there is no show.
  • If your attention wanders with someone, listen harder; that calls out the interesting.

From lecture section 16: Exceed the Righteousness of the Scribes and Pharisees (Matthew 5:20)

  • You must be more moral than a priest and more moral than a lawyer.
  • Corrupt lawyers subvert justice by using the law, justified with Luciferian rationalism.
  • 'Point to what I did wrong' can hide an unclean spirit.
  • The presumption of innocence is miraculous, and hard to apply even to yourself.

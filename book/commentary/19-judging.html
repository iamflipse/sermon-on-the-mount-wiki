Peterson's reading in plain words

Matthew 7 opens with "Judge not, that ye be not judged." Peterson notes that it does not exactly say "do not judge"; it says the measure you use will be used on you. Criticism in its real sense means separating the wheat from the chaff, not tearing things down. Any ideal judges you, and you can close the gap either by moving toward the good or by trying to destroy it, as Cain did with Abel.

The mote and the beam: students who dismiss great thinkers over one flaw are ignoring the beam in their own eye. Even a harsh critic can hand you the useful five percent if you listen. Few people, he quotes Carl Rogers, have the moral courage to listen. Clear your own vision first; only then can you help someone else see.

Key ideas

From lecture section 36: Judge Not, That Ye Be Not Judged (Matthew 7:1-2)

  • It does not exactly say do not judge; Revelation's Christ judges most harshly those who play both ends against the middle.
  • Read to learn: criticism means separating the wheat from the chaff, not denigration; read Freud despite Victorian assumptions.
  • Noah was 'a wise man in his generations': as good as a person could be for his time.
  • Jung: dreams compensate for gaps in conscious understanding; categories loosen in dreams.
  • Any ideal is a judge; you close the gap by moving toward the good or by destroying the good, as Cain did.
  • Without an ideal there is only anxiety and no joy; humility in faith mediates between you and the ideal.

From lecture section 37: The Mote and the Beam (Matthew 7:3-5)

  • Feeling morally superior to Nietzsche is a convenient accomplishment for a 19-year-old.
  • Even if criticism is 95 percent vitriol, the 5 percent may fix something you would not otherwise fix.
  • Your most vicious enemy can be your best friend because they go after subtle things.
  • Talk to people who think differently and ask how they came to their view.
  • Rogers: people generally don't have the moral courage to listen.
  • You cannot act as a moral social agent until you clear your own vision.

Questions to explore

  • What ideal judges you most harshly, and are you moving toward it or against it?
  • What could you keep from a thinker you dismissed?
  • What is the beam in your own eye?
  • Which critic of yours deserves a careful listen for the 5 percent?

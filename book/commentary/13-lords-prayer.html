Peterson's reading in plain words

Peterson walks through the prayer line by line. Heaven may be the place you are in when you are doing the right thing at the right time. To hallow something is to put it in the highest place, and if nothing is sacred you have no priorities, which feels like anxiety. "Thy kingdom come" is the moment when time disappears because you are fully absorbed; the task is to spend more of your life there.

"Thy will be done" means letting the highest value run all the way down, from God to community to family to you. What you most need is often found where you least want to look, and challenges you choose feel completely different from ones forced on you. "Forgive us our debts as we forgive our debtors" is reciprocity, not blanket absolution. "Lead us not into temptation" is humility about your limits: if you are quitting drinking, skip the party. The closing verses on forgiveness tie your inner judge, your conscience, to how you treat others.

Key ideas

From lecture section 28: Our Father, Hallowed Be Thy Name, Thy Kingdom Come (Matthew 6:9-10)

  • Heaven may be the spirit that shines through every level of a properly ordered hierarchy.
  • Even severe depression varies; find when you are less miserable and practice being there.
  • There is nothing worse than traveling efficiently down the wrong road.
  • If everything is equally important, everything is equally meaningless and you are anxious.

From lecture section 29: Thy Will Be Done, Daily Bread, Forgive Us Our Debts (Matthew 6:10-12)

  • Thy will be done means propagating the highest value down from God to state, city, community, family and individual.
  • If your life is trivial and you are bored, you are not doing anything meaningful.
  • Your salvation may be found in your problems: what you most need is where you least want to look.
  • Horrible things find you wherever you hide, and we are an unbroken chain of creatures who managed the worst.

From lecture section 30: Lead Us Not Into Temptation: The Desert and the Zone (Matthew 6:13)

  • Pray not to face temptations beyond your ability to control; know where not to go.
  • Abandoning your tyrannical presuppositions takes you into the desert, not straight to the promised land.
  • Up the mountain, down into the valley, up a higher mountain: no mountain to mountain without chaos.
  • If you stay open to incremental improvement, revelations become small, interesting revolutions.

From lecture section 31: If You Forgive Men Their Trespasses, and Fasting (Matthew 6:14-18)

  • Your conscience calls you out and you cannot simply tell it what to do.
  • The Holy Spirit may manifest as conscience, the judge within.
  • If you are harshly judgmental to raise your own moral status, your conscience will torture you in precise proportion.
  • You can get to love of yourself through love of others, for example by treating yourself well for your kids' sake.
  • Forgiveness is not the absence of judgment; walk the child through what was right and what to improve.
  • Letting kids rule at three is demoralizing; show them adulthood is better.
  • Peter Pan keeps his magic but never gets Wendy.

Questions to explore

  • When does your sense of time disappear?
  • Where are you most afraid to look?
  • What temptation should you simply avoid?
  • Whom are you judging harshly, and how does your conscience treat you?

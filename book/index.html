Sermon on the Mount — Complete Text with Jordan Peterson's Commentary

The Sermon on the Mount, with Jordan Peterson's Commentary

The complete text of Matthew 5–7 (all 111 verses) in the King James Version and the World English Bible, paired passage by passage with a plain-language guide to Jordan B. Peterson's lecture series on the sermon.

Start with the Cheat Sheet: the core idea, seven main points, seven practices, and a one-minute version.

What this is

A study wiki. The Bible text is public domain and is published here in full. The commentary is a set of summaries, key ideas and short quotes drawn from a transcript of Peterson's four-day lecture series (about 81,000 words), merged into 27 passage pages that follow the sermon's own order. The lecture transcript itself is not published.

How to use it

  • Read the text. Matthew 5, Matthew 6 and Matthew 7 give every verse with the KJV and WEB side by side. Each block of verses links to its commentary.
  • Read a passage. Each commentary page has the verses in full, Peterson's reading in plain words, key ideas, two to four short quotes, questions to explore, and related links.
  • Search by modern words. Themes maps words like resilience, anxiety, purpose and boundaries to the passages where the lecture deals with them, even when it never uses the word.
  • Check the sources. The verse map shows which lecture sections feed each page and the five verses the lecture never reads aloud. About lists sources and licences.

Use the search icon (top left) or press S to search the whole book.

Peterson's reading in one paragraph

Peterson reads the sermon line by line as practical psychology, not as a list of doctrines. It begins on a mountain because the first rule is to aim up, at the highest good you can conceive. From there: admit what you do not know, tell the truth, and take on burdens voluntarily, because that is what builds you. Judgment moves from outcome to intent, so anger and lust count, and reconciliation comes before worship. Do good, pray and fast in secret rather than for status. Put one aim at the top and stop worrying. Clear your own eye before judging anyone else, ask for what you want, and treat others as you want to be treated, going first. Faith is shown in what you do, not what you say, and the house built on rock is the character that has already faced catastrophe before it arrives.

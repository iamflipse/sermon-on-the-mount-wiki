## Peterson's reading in plain words

No one can serve two masters. Peterson reads this as a call to one integrated aim at the top of your values, rather than several that pull against each other. Mammon means wealth and the desire for security, but he stresses that this is not an anti-capitalist teaching, and not a call to misery either: Christ turns water into wine at a wedding, and paradise may be a feast. Constraints you choose are there to make you disciplined, productive and generous, and real mastery shows itself in being able to handle even grim things with humour.

## Key ideas

- One master means a pyramid with an integrated pinnacle.
- Mammon is wealth and the desire for security; this is not anti-capitalist.
- Jews celebrate life more abundant; Christ makes wine and goes to weddings; paradise may be a banquet or eternal play.
- Constraints voluntarily accepted are so you become disciplined, productive and generous.
- Lecturing on atrocity at Harvard, Peterson heard a voice: if you mastered this, you could do it with humor.

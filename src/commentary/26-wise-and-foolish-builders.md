## Peterson's reading in plain words

What is the rock? Peterson answers: the crucifixion, the full acceptance of tragedy and malice, and building a character strong enough to withstand it. It works like exposure therapy. Walk through the worst before it happens and you are ready when it comes, and perhaps it then does not happen at all.

He has seen people face the death of someone they love bravely: they still suffer the loss, but it is not hell. Sorting things out with your parents before their last years, for example, lets you care for them without bitterness. Sand is scattered multiplicity, not unity. When the house falls, the question is whether the storm did it, or whether you were not ready. How prepared can you get?

## Key ideas

- The rock is the crucifixion and the passion: acceptance of tragedy and malevolence.
- Tool a character in response to that challenge that can withstand all of it.
- It is exposure: walk through it before it happens, be ready, and maybe it doesn't happen.
- Lose your life to find it: expose yourself voluntarily, a priori, to what you want to avoid.
- People who confront death bravely still suffer the loss, but it isn't hell; they transmute it.
- Sort things out with your parents before their final stages and you can shepherd them without bitterness.
- Sand is multiplicity, not unity.
- Did the house fall because of the storm or because it was built on sand? How prepared can you get, even for the apocalypse?

## Peterson's reading in plain words

Ask and it shall be given: the chance of getting what you want without asking is close to zero. Peterson says many people hide what they want in a fog so they never have to risk asking, and then blame others for missing a target they never named. Seeking means watching yourself honestly, noticing when you are less miserable and doing more of that, and following what beckons.

"If his son ask bread, will he give him a stone?" leads him to biology. You are adapted to the world well enough to be alive, so opening yourself to learning should work, but you will not find out unless you go all in; faith has to come before the evidence. A small request, like asking a new neighbour a favour, opens relationships. "If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts" becomes the idea that each person is a small model of the cosmos, and that a sincere question tends to receive an answer.

## Key ideas

**From lecture section 39: Ask, Seek, Knock** (Matthew 7:7-8)

- A halo is a sun or moon behind the figure of value; the crown of thorns may be a solar image.
- Christ knocks at the door like the sin crouching at Cain's: do you open it?
- 'Do Not Hide Unwanted Things in the Fog': people will not let themselves know what they want.
- Being vague about wants lets you punish your partner for missing a target they never saw.

**From lecture section 40: If His Son Asks Bread, Will He Give Him a Stone? Adaptation and Faith** (Matthew 7:9-10)

- The question is whether you can trust the fundamental substructure of reality.
- Biologically, you are adapted at least enough to live and reproduce, so learning and transformation should work.
- You are a repertoire of biologically instantiated possibility; the gym shows capacity drawn out in proportion to effort.
- The upper limit is unknown; people who seem to do six impossible things maximize their potential.

**From lecture section 41: Good Gifts, the Microcosm, and the Yoga Client** (Matthew 7:11)

- Dawkins sent Peterson a paper saying an organism must be a microcosm of its environment.
- From a bird you could infer air, gravity and temperature on Earth.
- If you were not a microcosm of the cosmos you could not live in it; that is both a biological and a religious answer.
- Kenosis means emptying: Christ as a thumbnail of God, bounded in time and place as a carpenter.

## Questions to explore

- What do you want that you have hidden in the fog?
- What beckons to you that you have not followed?
- What capacity in you has never been asked for, the way a muscle is in the gym?
- Who could you ask for a small favor to start a relationship?
- If someone studied you, what world would they infer you were built for?
- Where in your body are you still braced for an old impact?

## Peterson's reading in plain words

Peterson treats vision and attention as primary. What you aim at decides what you see: name your aim and the world lays itself out as paths that help you and obstacles that get in the way, with feelings to match. Positive emotion says "path open"; negative emotion says "stop" or "danger".

Sin, in the Greek, is an archery word meaning to miss the target, or to have no target at all. If the eye is single, aimed at the good, the whole body is full of light. If the eye is evil, the darkness has no bottom: Hitler, he says, ended up exactly where he was aiming.

## Key ideas

- Primates with the best vision live among the most poisonous snakes; we see color to find ripe fruit.
- Thought follows attention; attention is primary and reveals your hierarchy of values.
- Specify your aim and the world reveals affordances and obstacles; objects are what object.
- Positive emotion means pathway open; negative emotion means stop or danger.
- Sin, hamartia, is an archery term: to miss the target, or to have none.
- The eye on the pyramid is not part of the pyramid; what is at the top is above the hierarchy.
- Human eye whites signal attention; a shared vision aligns people so they can compete without conflict.
- Hitler ended exactly where he aimed; however miserable you are, there is a stupid thing that can make it worse.

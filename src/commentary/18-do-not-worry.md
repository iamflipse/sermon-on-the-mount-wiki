## Peterson's reading in plain words

"Consider the lilies" can sound like a drop-out ethic: stop working and drift. Peterson says the passage turns on faith instead. Obsessive worry about security, and even an obsessive sense of duty, can reflect a lack of faith.

The answer is in "seek ye first the kingdom": aim at the highest possible good in every way, and the rest is added. Your real interest stretches across time and includes other people. If your aim is right and your words are true, you can stop worrying and give full attention to the present, trusting that the larger picture is in order.

## Key ideas

**From lecture section 34: No Man Can Serve Two Masters, Consider the Lilies** (Matthew 6:24-30)

- The lilies do not toil, but the passage turns on faith.

**From lecture section 35: Seek Ye First the Kingdom, Take No Thought for the Morrow** (Matthew 6:31-34)

- There are sins of conscientiousness: obsessive concern for security and duty.
- Seek first the kingdom: aim at the highest possible good in every possible way.
- Kant: act so that if everyone did the same, it would be the best outcome.
- Your best interest is across time and includes everyone.
- Aim in the spirit of love, serve it with truth, and you can say what you believe without worry.
- Letting go of your life is how you get to have it.

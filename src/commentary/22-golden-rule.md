## Peterson's reading in plain words

"Whatsoever ye would that men should do to you, do ye even so to them: for this is the law and the prophets." Peterson calls it a direct instruction: work out exactly how you would like to be treated, then do that to others first. That is the price. If you treat others in the best possible way, you are already doing the best thing, so in a sense you win the game.

He then widens it. Life will bring suffering, so the better stance is not grudging acceptance but "celebratory acceptance: bring it on." Joy and suffering may be tied together, and a great adventure may be better than mere happiness. A garden with a snake may even be a better garden, because you learn how to handle snakes. The golden rule turns tradition into a living principle of careful, judicious reciprocity.

## Key ideas

**From lecture section 42: Do Unto Others: The Law and the Prophets, Ultimate Sacrifice** (Matthew 7:12)

- 'How good could things get if you put yourself together?'
- The law is custom and tradition, Osiris, necessary, wise and dead; it must ally with the prophetic voice.
- Multiple characterizations of the divine imply a central essence; Christ names it as the golden rule.
- Figure out how you would like to be treated, then do it to others first; it is a direct instruction.
- If you treat others in the best possible manner you are already doing the best thing: you win the game.
- Most parents would die for their children; that is the fundamental relationship.

**From lecture section 43: Bring It On: Joy and Suffering, Adventure, the Serpent and the Tyrant** (Matthew 7:12 (continued))

- You are going to face all of it, so accept it: 'celebratory acceptance. Bring it on.'
- Nietzsche: imagine joy and suffering are proportionate and tied together.
- Profound music like Beethoven's Ninth goes from the tragic depths to the pinnacle; mere happiness is insipid elevator music.
- God calls Abraham to war, famine and tyranny, yet it is a great adventure, maybe better than happy.
- The pirate friend, a lone round-the-world sailor, is hired to fire narcissists in corrupt corporations and enjoys it; better than a nice guy.
- Do you want to be harmless or something to contend with? You do not get that without accepting the whole catastrophe.

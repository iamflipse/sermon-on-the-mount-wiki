## Peterson's reading in plain words

This is not a condemnation of wealth, Peterson insists. Despising abundance is foolish when much of the world still lacks it. But money does not solve the deep problems of life: your marriage, your parents' decline, your own death.

The treasure moth and rust cannot touch is character, which protects you better than wealth and also generates it. Hunter-gatherers stored future food in their reputations by sharing their kills and playing down their luck, and that kind of treasure does not rot or get stolen. You will often face a choice between character and wealth, and where your treasure is shows in which one you pick.

## Key ideas

- Being rich does not fix your marriage, death, or your parents' decline.
- You are a fool if you despise abundance; a billion people still burn dung.
- Character is a better protection against catastrophe than wealth, and conscientiousness predicts success.
- Sacrificing character for wealth is a bad trade, because you want wealth to get what only character delivers.
- Surrounded by people who love you, you ride out political and social chaos best.
- Good hunters downplay their luck and give away the best cuts, storing future food in reputation.
- Reputation does not rot and is not stolen.
- Where your treasure is: you will choose between character and wealth often, maybe daily.

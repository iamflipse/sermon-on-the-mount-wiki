## Peterson's reading in plain words

Matthew 6 opens with a warning against doing good to be seen, which Peterson calls an injunction against virtue signalling. Be at least a little sceptical of your own motives. Young people feel a real urge to save the world, he says, but they are often told to do it by protesting before they have disciplined themselves. His favourite definition of Christian charity is productivity plus generosity.

Not letting your left hand know what your right hand is doing means keeping good deeds secret even from yourself, so you do not puff yourself up or let your status rise beyond your competence. He spends time on how narcissists and psychopaths fake goodness to win reputation, and warns that naivety dressed up as virtue is an invitation to exploitation.

## Key ideas

**From lecture section 24: Do Not Your Alms Before Men: Virtue Signaling** (Matthew 6:1)

- The 'do not pray in public' injunction is an injunction against virtue signaling.
- Be skeptical of your own motives; do good in secret to avoid moral hazard.
- Piaget: morality emerges from repeated games, and late adolescence brings a messianic urge to find your place.
- Peer groups are a bridge from parents to autonomy; a star who is not a team player is a skilled narcissist.
- Students waste a median of 8 hours a day, roughly $150,000 a year at $50 an hour, yet are told to be moral avatars.
- The best definition of Christian charity: productivity and generosity.

**From lecture section 25: Let Not Thy Left Hand Know: Narcissism, Psychopathy and Naivety** (Matthew 6:2-5)

- Do not let your status rise beyond your competence.
- Keep good deeds secret even from yourself, because you will aggrandize yourself.
- Psychopathy is not a path to success: failure to learn from experience, short-term hedonic priority.
- Psychopaths betray themselves as much as others and become drifters because people warn each other.
- At protests, a few men are there to exploit the young women: serious predators.
- About 1 percent of criminals commit about 70 percent of crimes.

## Questions to explore

- When you do good, who are you hoping notices?
- How many hours a day do you waste, and what are they worth?
- Where is your naivety willful blindness?
- What good do you do that you would still do if no one knew?

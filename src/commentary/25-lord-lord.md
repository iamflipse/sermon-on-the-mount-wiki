## Peterson's reading in plain words

Saying "Lord, Lord" is not enough. Peterson says faith is not mainly a statement about what you believe; it is in the doing, acted out in the world. That is why Christianity stresses incarnation, the word taking on flesh, and why Christ is a carpenter, as hands-on as it gets.

Pointing to your prophesying and good works is not enough either, especially if those works are really for your own glory. The kingdom, he says, is a real psychological and social state you can live in, and the sermon is a practical guide to getting there.

## Key ideas

- Modern rationalists think you just have to say that you believe.
- Saying it doesn't doom you, but it is no certain indication of salvation.
- The fundamental essence is 'but he that doeth': an existential emphasis, the imitation of Christ.
- The word takes on determinate form: incarnation; Christ is a carpenter, as hands-on as it gets.
- Pointing to your achievements as evidence of ethical wondrousness is the temptation of the aristocracy.
- The kingdom of God is a psychological and social state you can actually inhabit; this is a practical guide.
- Mere propositional faith fails, and so does piling up good works for your own benefit.

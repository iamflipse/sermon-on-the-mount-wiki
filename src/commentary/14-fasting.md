## Peterson's reading in plain words

Peterson reads the fasting verses as a warning against false martyrdom: "look how much I am suffering for you." That kind of display blames others for your sacrifices and then uses them as proof of moral superiority. The hypocrites "have their reward", and he notes the line is a joke: the reward is only the attention.

The alternative is to wash your face and look put-together when you are giving something up. Like giving and prayer, fasting is turned inward, done for the Father who sees in secret rather than for an audience.

## Key ideas

**From lecture section 31: If You Forgive Men Their Trespasses, and Fasting** (Matthew 6:14-18)

- Do not exploit evidence of your suffering to climb the heavenly hierarchy: the false martyr.

**Also in this passage**

- Anointing your head and washing your face means looking put-together while you make a sacrifice.
- This continues the chapter's turn from the social and public to the private and internal.

## Peterson's reading in plain words

Peterson reads the adultery verses as a second, harsher example of the shift from outcome to intent. If you would sleep around but cannot because no one wants you, the absence of the act is no proof of virtue. He leaves it to the listener to decide whether that standard is right, but he is clear that this is what the line means.

"If thy right eye offend thee, pluck it out" he reads as sacrificing a part to save the whole. Everyone runs a model of the world, and learning means letting part of that model die. Often the thing you value most is the biggest obstacle to moving forward, which is why the example is the right eye and the right hand. His summary: die a little all the time, so you do not have to die a lot, or completely.

## Key ideas

**From lecture section 20: If Thy Right Eye Offend Thee, and Divorce** (Matthew 5:29-32)

- You always run a model of the world; learning means retooling it, so no progress without death and repentance.
- Often what you value most is the biggest impediment; that is why the example is the right eye and right hand.
- Die a little all the time so you do not die a lot or completely.
- Thought is a sacrificial process: create avatars of yourself and let them die instead of you.
- Out of the tyranny you go into the desert, which is not obviously better.

**Also in this passage**

- Looking with lust is judged like the act itself: the same move from outcome to intent as the verses on anger.
- Not sleeping around because no one is interested is not virtue if the desire is still there.

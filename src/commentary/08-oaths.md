## Peterson's reading in plain words

"Swear not at all" is not about bad language. Peterson reads it as a warning against claiming to stand for moral principles you cannot actually carry. Be wary of anyone who swears he acts on the highest principle. A more honest stance is: here is what I am aiming at, I am probably wrong somewhere, point it out.

Letting your yes be yes and your no be no is a call to simple speech, offering neither more than you can deliver nor nothing. In an argument, he adds, work out what would actually satisfy you, and ask the other person the same. He ends with a warning about loud claims to speak for the poor: poverty is rarely caused only by a lack of money.

## Key ideas

- Do not swear by God that you will do good; who are you to carry that burden?
- You should distrust someone who swears he acts on the highest principle.
- Better: 'Here's what I'm aiming at. I'm probably an idiot. Point it out.'
- In an argument, figure out what would satisfy you, and ask the other person.
- Simplicity of speech: do not offer more than you can, but not nothing.
- Poverty is rarely caused by lack of money; a client was fine until his disability check brought predatory friends.

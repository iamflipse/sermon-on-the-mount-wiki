## Peterson's reading in plain words

"An eye for an eye" sounds brutal, but Peterson points out that in its time it was a limit: take no more than was taken. Christ pushes further with turning the other cheek and going the extra mile.

He treats the extra mile as a practical rule that keeps working when it is repeated, though not to the point of martyrdom. In business it means delivering faster, better and more reliably than expected, and never annoying your customers. A critic may be a canary in the coal mine, warning you of something real, and an enemy you win over often becomes your best ally. How do you know your enemy is not trying to improve you?

## Key ideas

- Proportional retaliation was a real limit on vengefulness.
- Going the extra mile is an extremely useful iterative ethical principle, though not to the point of martyrdom.
- In business: deliver faster, at a reasonable price, at higher quality than expected.
- Rule number one: do not annoy your customers; one annoyed customer can cost a million people's goodwill.
- A rude video store owner went out of business in three months after Peterson's disagreeable friend called him out.
- A complaining customer may be a canary in the coal mine; respond and you may convert him.
- An enemy converted is a better ally; how do you know your enemy is not trying to improve you?

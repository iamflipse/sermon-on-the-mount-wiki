## Peterson's reading in plain words

Before he reads a single blessing, Peterson asks why the sermon is given on a mountain. His answer is that height is the oldest symbol of seeing far and aiming high. From the top you can see where you are going, so the first rule is simple: aim up.

He argues that most good feeling in life comes from seeing yourself make progress toward a worthwhile goal, not from reaching it. Without an aim, and an ethical one, you cannot feel much that is positive at all. Since you are betting your life on what you do anyway, you might as well bet it on the highest good you can imagine. He links this to taking on heavy responsibility on purpose, which he says is the real source of self-respect, and to taking on challenges voluntarily, which builds you up rather than turning you into something that hides.

## Key ideas

- Enlightenment happens on a mountain, not in a valley, because from above you can see; the eye of Horus, the falcon and Zazu in The Lion King carry the same symbol.
- The word that is true and oriented by love brings habitable order out of chaos, and people participate in that with their own speech.
- Most positive emotion comes from seeing progress toward a goal, not from attaining it; without an ethical aim you cannot experience positive emotion.
- God classically defined as the summum bonum, the sum of all good things, is a definition, not a statement of belief.
- You are betting your life on your actions anyway, so why bet it on anything less than the highest good.
- Bearing a heavy responsibility is the only real answer to the burden of your own flaws, and service to others is one of the deepest pleasures of life.
- Voluntary challenge turns on new genes and builds new parts of you; fleeing a challenge turns you into a prey animal.
- Betrayal, death and old age are coming, so you had better be set up for the challenge.

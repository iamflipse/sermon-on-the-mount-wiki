## Peterson's reading in plain words

"Do not give what is holy to dogs" means do not waste what is precious on someone not ready for it. Peterson reads it as a lesson in listening. If someone is not taking in what you say, you are probably pitching it at the wrong level, or you are not talking to who you think you are: perhaps to a frightened ten-year-old part of them, or to your own projection.

So stop talking. In therapy he lets people speak until they are ready to listen, and uses Rogers' method: summarise what they said until they agree you have it right. Tension drains away and the useless parts fall off. Healing, he says, is not just letting feelings out; it is updating your map of the world so the past does not repeat.

## Key ideas

- 'Do not cast pearls before swine' means do not waste what is holy on what is unfit for it.
- When troubled people do not take advice, you are pitching above their position.
- If someone is not listening, you are not where you think you are and not talking to who you think.
- Partial subpersonalities: sometimes you are talking to a part of someone that is ten years old.
- A client needed to talk 45 minutes per session; over ten years that dropped to 15 and she was doing stand-up comedy.
- If someone is not listening, shut up; doubling down never works clinically.
- Rogers: listen, then summarize to their satisfaction; tension drains and chaff falls away.
- Healing is not catharsis; it is updating the map so you are prepared and the past does not repeat.
